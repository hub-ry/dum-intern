Docs

Dum is a Mac app you delegate to. It sits on your screen as a small floating circle with Dum's face. It follows what you're learning across your goals, keeps track of the skills you've shown you have, and writes code when you tell it to, but only with those skills. The point is that you could take the AI away and still understand the architecture, start implementing, and ask precise questions.

The loop goes like this. You say what you need done. Dum checks that you and it agree on what your goal means. The Wizard lays out a few things you could hand off now, and marks the ones you'd have to learn first. You choose one, check the handoff, and press Do this. Dum does it, you review the result, and all of it goes on the session's trail.

Two characters talk to you. Dum is the intern you work with. The Wizard helps you decide when you're choosing a direction or the next thing to hand off. It doesn't chime in otherwise.

Getting started

See installation. Dum needs macOS 13 or later. Voice needs macOS 14 or later on Apple Silicon; everything else works from the keyboard on Intel too.

Dum has no Dock icon and no menu bar icon. On first launch Dum's panel opens and asks What do you want to work toward? Type an answer or say it. It becomes your first goal: the answer is the goal's text, and its first few words are the name, which you can change later. Nothing is sent to a model for this.

Because the look is on by default, macOS asks for Screen Recording permission the first time Dum looks. You can say no; see the look for what changes.

Next, Dum needs someone to power it. A banner says Dum isn't powered yet with a Who powers Dum? button. Goals, skills, notes and commands like :tree work without a backend. Messages to Dum, goal alignment, decision cards, :projects and :submit don't. Your goal is kept, and alignment picks up once Dum is powered. See who powers Dum.

The circle, the column and the panels

The circle floats above your other apps, on every Space. Click it and it unfolds into a column of up to seven circles on one translucent capsule:

  • Dum, with Dum's face: your goals.
  • Up to three goals: the ones you pinned, or with none pinned the three most recently updated, the active one first. Each shows its progress, and a mark while it has a step.
  • The skill tree.
  • The Monitor: whether Dum is recording, and what it noticed.
  • Settings.

Click a circle and the column folds into it and opens that circle's panel. The panel's top-left corner sits at the circle, or flips left or up when there's no room. The column folds back by itself after 8 seconds without the pointer over it, and Esc folds it too. The Open Dum shortcut, ⌘⇧D by default, opens or hides the panel from anywhere.

Drag the circle to move it. Dum remembers one spot per display and puts the circle back there next time. To move the circle without a mouse, use Move circle in Settings: the arrow keys move it 10 points, Shift and an arrow 1 point, Return keeps it there and Esc puts it back. You can also pick a display from the list.

The face tells you what Dum is doing: idle, looking, thinking, listening while you talk, or a ! badge when something needs you, such as a decision waiting, a rejected API key, a voice error, or Dum's background process having stopped. A dashed ring means the look is paused. The circle is a labeled button, and its label says the same thing in words.

The panels

Every panel uses the same dark theme.

  • Dum: What do you want to work toward? and your goals as a nested list, each with its progress and step. Typing a goal makes it, opens it and pins it to the column while fewer than three are pinned. Click a row to open the goal; the pin and skip icons show when you hover over it.
  • A goal: its name and progress, the step cloud, the chat with a one-line message box, and a Path strip of the goal's skills with ▶ on each. Stop shows only while Dum is working. More has Edit goal, New goal inside, Delete goal, Suggested projects, Changes and Memory.
  • Skill tree: Graph or Tree, a track filter, Tidy and ▶ Play; see skills.
  • Monitor: a big status, ● Recording, Paused or Not recording: and why; the look model, the last check, whether pictures are on, and Pause/Resume. Under it, the live context log: notes about what you're doing, app switches, looks that were skipped and why, errors, pauses and the Wizard's chimes. The log is kept in memory only, up to 200 entries.
  • Settings: see Settings.

A goal's records and its editor open over the goal panel; Esc returns to where you were. Switching to another app hides the panel. Esc backs out one step at a time, then hides the panel; ⌘W hides it straight away. Both give focus back to the app you were in. Your draft stays.

The bubble

The bubble is a thought cloud with small puffs trailing toward the circle. It has no buttons, you can't click it, and clicks go through to whatever is under it.

  • The step. Beside the circle, the active goal's step and the goal's name. It doesn't time out: it stays until the step is done, skipped or replaced, and hides only while Dum's panel is visible and focused.
  • The Wizard. When the look sees you stuck, such as the same error again, undoing and redoing, searching the same thing again, or staring at a failing test, the Wizard's one-sentence nudge shows beside the circle in a purple cloud for 20 seconds. It chimes at most once a minute, and every chime also goes in the Monitor's log.
  • Replies. Near your cursor, every reply from Dum, typed or spoken, and every finished Do this, unless Dum's panel is visible and focused. It's one sentence of what Dum said, plus one line from the Wizard when the Wizard spoke, and it stays 15 seconds. A question Dum asks you shows as that question; a decision card waiting for you shows A decision is waiting for you in Dum. The full reply is in the goal's chat.
  • Voice. Near your cursor, Listening…, Transcribing… and your text.

Steps, skipping and trust

Every goal has exactly one next step, one sentence. Dum works it out from where the goal stands, in this order:

  1. Say what finishing this goal looks like. (alignment)
  2. Pick a project theme or idea.
  3. The chosen project's next milestone.
  4. The next skill on the goal's path: Explain what X is for, in your own words, then Write X yourself, without AI.

The goal's step cloud has a Pick … for me button, such as Pick the project idea for me, which sends Dum a fixed request as if you typed it, and Skip. When the step is a skill, that skill's graph card has Pick … for me too. A step goes away once it's done.

There's always a way to skip. Skipping a skill step trusts that skill: it's added to your tree as your word that you can write it, at build level, the same as :skill. It counts for the gate, so Dum may write it for you, and it shows as trusted with a dashed ring, never as proven. Beginner skills skip in one click; others ask Sure? Dum will treat X as known and write it for you. Skipping a whole goal, with the skip icon in the Dum panel and a confirm, trusts every skill on its path you don't hold yet, prerequisites first; Undo skip clears the mark and leaves the trusted skills. Undo trust on a skill's card removes it.

▶ on a skill, in the Path strip or on a graph card, makes it the active goal's step. ▶ Play in the skill tree picks the next one for you and says why: Next: X. followed by the reason.

Delegating

Goal alignment

Every goal starts with alignment, so you and Dum agree on what it means. It's the goal's first step, and you can skip it like any other. It runs when you create a goal and when you change a goal's text. Renaming a goal doesn't count. If you edit a goal you're not in, the alignment card names that goal and leaves the one you're in alone.

  1. Dum reflects your goal back as something you want to become able to do.
  2. It asks at most two questions, and only ones whose answer changes the plan. Each says why it matters. Answer in the card.
  3. The Wizard offers directions to choose from. Each is a learning project or a decision, with the skills it builds, why it advances your goal, its tradeoff, and Based on: the context it used.

Use this direction or Use my own direction opens a short form: what you'll be able to do, how you'll know it worked, and any assumptions you accept. Agree this direction saves it. Revise asks again; Not now puts it off. Current context shows Alignment needed until you agree one.

A direction records intent. It writes no file, isn't a schedule, and doesn't let Dum do anything it couldn't before. Chat, Settings and commands you're allowed to give all work without one.

Decision cards

Say what you need done next and press Help me decide. Dum restates your outcome, may ask a question, and the Wizard compares two or three ways to get there. Each option names the task, the expected result, what you'll review, the skills it needs, why it helps and its tradeoff, and what it's based on. Dum, not the model, checks each option against your skills and labels it:

  • Can delegate now. Choose turns it into a handoff.
  • Learn first. It needs a skill you haven't proven yet. Suggest a project to learn it asks for suggested projects for that skill.
  • Needs a detail. Something it can't guess. Give this detail puts you in the message box.

Revise asks again, Use my own plan puts you in the message box, and Dismiss clears the card. A dismissed card doesn't come back until you ask again. You can always ignore the cards and type your own request.

The handoff

Choosing an option makes a handoff card with three fields: Task, Expected result and What you'll review. Choosing writes nothing. Edit changes the fields, Dismiss drops it, and Do this tells Dum to do it.

  • Nothing runs until you press Do this, and a handoff runs once.
  • If your goal, direction, notes or session changed since you chose it, the card says Needs refresh; Refresh handoff rebinds it to what's current.
  • If the message box has unsent text, the card says so. Do this never sends it along.
  • Every file Dum writes goes through the same gate and save check as any other change. A skill taken back after you chose stops it.
  • ⌘. stops it. Changes already made stay listed with Revert.

Review

When it's done the card shows what happened and each change's diff, with Revert and open the change record. Reviewed… asks whether it met the expected result and saves your verdict on the session's trail. A verdict can be that it didn't help. It never counts as evidence of a skill.

Sessions, the trail and the story

Dum keeps a record of how you work in each goal. The panels don't show these records right now; they stay on disk, and the Monitor shows what the look noticed as it happens.

Sessions

A session is one stretch of work in one goal. It starts when you're active in the goal and ends when you leave the goal, quit, or go 30 minutes without activity. Changing what Dum works from also starts a new one: the mode, the backend, the goal's text, language or notes, personal context, an agreed direction, or a correction.

The trail

A trail is the skills a session visited, in order. Coming back to a skill after leaving it is a new visit, marked as a revisit. A line between two visits means "came next", not "is a prerequisite of". Agreed directions and each handoff step (commanded, then done, blocked, failed, cancelled or interrupted, then reviewed) are marked on it. Something Dum couldn't place on your tree is kept as a gap. A visit isn't credit: only evidence moves a skill.

The story

The story reads your sessions goal by goal. It's a cache over the saved sessions, kept in ~/.dum/zones/<zone>/story/, and the sessions stay the record.

Goals

A goal is something you're learning, like Programming › Data Structures. It's not a folder or a Git repository. Goals are the only scope Dum has.

  • Each goal has a name, its text in your words, and an optional language. Leave the language empty and the goal uses its parent's.
  • Goals nest, up to 16 levels deep. Names must differ between siblings.
  • The Dum panel lists them all, nested. A goal's More menu has Edit goal, New goal inside and Delete goal. There's no way to move a goal to a new parent.
  • A goal can have notes: background Dum reads in this goal and the goals inside it, up to 16 KiB per goal, in ~/.dum/zones/<zone>/context.md. Notes are context. They never unlock a skill or give Dum access to a file.

Opening a goal makes it the active context. Do it from the column, the Dum panel or with ⌘K. Switching stops whatever Dum was doing in the old goal and ends its session. Earlier conversation in the new goal comes back as history; a question Dum asked last time isn't waiting for an answer any more.

A goal inherits its ancestors' text, nearest language and notes, up to 64 KiB of notes in total, keeping the nearest goals first. It doesn't inherit their conversation, memory, direction, handoffs, sessions, suggested projects, change history, followed folders or step skips. Those belong to one goal. Your skills belong to none: there's one tree, and a skill you prove in one goal counts in all of them.

Deleting a goal also deletes the goals inside it. Your skills, memory notes and evidence stay, and the goal's files stay on disk in ~/.dum/zones/ for you to look at.

Skills and evidence

Your skills are plain Markdown files in ~/.dum/skills/, one per skill. You can read, edit or delete them; they open in Obsidian. A skill is scoped to a language or to none, so printing in Python doesn't count in Go.

The skill tree circle opens your curated tracks: basics for C, C++, Go, Java, JavaScript, Python, Rust and TypeScript, plus graphics, interview, systems and a language-free builder track. Each skill shows as built, recognized, open (everything under it is built) or locked, and a skill you skipped also shows as trusted. Skills you add that aren't on a track still get up to three mapped prerequisites.

Graph lays the skills out left to right by prerequisite depth, one band per track. Drag the background to pan, scroll or pinch to zoom, and drag a node; it springs back toward its place, and Tidy puts everything back. Built is filled, recognized half-filled, open outlined with a glow, locked dim with a lock, trusted has a dashed ring and a trusted tag, and the next skill pulses. Arrow keys walk the graph, Return opens a skill's card and Esc closes it. The card has ▶ Work on this, Skip, trust me and Undo trust. Tree is the rung list with your progress and the web tree link.

Recognize, build, apply

  • Recognize. You explained what the skill is and what it's for, in your own words. Dum quotes the sentence it's crediting, and the quote has to come from your message in that request. Its prerequisites have to be recognized too.
  • Build. You wrote it yourself, unaided. Share the files, Dum reviews them, then asks whether you wrote them yourself without AI or copied code. Only y or yes records the build; anything else keeps the review and records nothing. When you hand in a suggested project, the --unaided flag is that yes. A build also needs its prerequisites built.
  • Apply. You built it and then reasoned about when and why to use it. The same reasoning without a build only records recognition.

Screens, files Dum reads, suggestions, memory, goal notes, trail visits, handoff reviews and code Dum wrote never count as evidence. A saved file doesn't prove you wrote it; your own yes does, and it can be wrong. Reviews are model judgments, so check them.

You can also add a skill you already have with :skill name or :skill name in python. That records a self-report that you can write it unaided, and it isn't reviewed. :skill -name takes a skill off the tree.

The gate

The gate decides what Dum may write for you. It's checked in code, not just asked of the model. Concepts (language features, data structures, algorithms) need build. Tools (one library, framework, API or command) need recognize. Skills on a curated track always count as concepts. Every prerequisite under a skill has to hold too, so if you take an early rung back, the skills that rest on it lock again, though their notes stay.

:boundary shows what Dum may do right now: what it may write, what it may use as a tool, what's closed, and which files it may read and change.

"not yet"

If Dum just credited you with something you don't think you've earned, say not yet, or not yet recursion to name the skill. The skill goes back to where it was, and Dum holds it there across goals and restarts. Only a reviewed unaided build or a :skill self-report lifts the hold.

The bar at the top of the skill tree's Tree view reads newbie, intern, good and cracked at 0, 8, 24 and 64 built skills with their prerequisites intact. Messages and recognition don't move it. It's a joke label, not a rating.

Talking to Dum

Type in the one-line message box at the bottom of a goal's chat. Return sends. Stop shows only while Dum is working. There's one conversation and one draft per goal.

Voice

Hold ⌃⌥Space and talk; let go to stop. Speech is turned into text on your Mac by a bundled copy of OpenSuperWhisper. Set it up once under Set up voice in Settings: allow the microphone (the prompt is attributed to Dum), then pick and download a speech model, because none is bundled. The install page has the steps. The text lands in your draft. It never sends itself.

While you use voice the bubble shows Listening…, then Transcribing…, then the draft for 20 seconds; press ⌘⇧↩ to send it, or open Dum to edit it. Dum's reply then shows in the bubble like any other reply (see the bubble).

  • Voice won't start over a draft that isn't empty. Send it, edit it or clear it first.
  • A recording stops after two minutes and is thrown away.
  • The audio is a temporary file, deleted when transcription finishes or fails.
  • You can also start and stop voice with the Voice button.
  • Voice comes with the packaged app (npm run desktop:mac) on Apple Silicon with macOS 14 or later. Running Dum straight from a checkout with npm run desktop has no voice.
  • If another app owns the shortcut, macOS refuses it silently and the press does nothing. Change the Hold-to-talk shortcut in Settings.

Keyboard only

Everything works without the mouse.

  • Return sends, Shift-Return adds a line, ⌘. stops Dum (or the debug chat, when your focus is in it).
  • Esc backs out one step: an open confirm, chooser, menu or form first, then an open record or the goal editor, then it hides the panel. On the column it folds the column. It never stops Dum, answers a question or dismisses a card.
  • ⌘K opens another goal and filters as you type. ⌘W hides the panel.
  • The skill graph: arrow keys walk along prerequisites and columns, Return opens a skill's card, Esc closes it.
  • The trail: arrow keys move between visits, Home and End jump.
  • The three global shortcuts (Open Dum, Hold to talk, Send the draft) can be changed in Settings. Each needs ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥.

Sharing

Dum can't see your files unless you show it. When you hand in a suggested project (More → Suggested projects), you pick the files or folder in a macOS dialog. A share lasts for one message: once Dum finishes answering, it can't read the files again. Text it already read stays in the conversation. A share you don't send expires after five minutes.

Commands

  • :tree or :skills: your skill tree.
  • :inspect file or :inspect file:10-40: show Dum a file you shared or follow.
  • :projects: suggested projects.
  • :submit p1 file --unaided: hand in your work on a project.
  • not yet: undo the skill just recorded.
  • :skill x, :skill -x: add or remove a skill.
  • :memory, :remember note: this goal's notes.
  • :context: the personal background used for suggestions.
  • :evidence: what each skill rests on.
  • :boundary: what Dum may do right now.
  • :log: everything said in this goal.
  • :help: this list.

Mode in Settings is understand or anti-vibe. Both use the same gate. Anti-vibe asks for your approach first, then Dum does the unlocked parts.

Voice, the circle's click-through corners and dragging, giving focus back to your previous app, and the circle and bubble over full-screen apps and other Spaces use macOS APIs that haven't been tried on a real Mac yet.

Changes

Ask Dum to change a file, or hand it off, and if you hold the skills it writes the change straight into the file. There's no yes/no step. You see the diff afterward, with a Revert button.

  • Dum only writes files you shared with the message or that sit in a folder you follow in this goal. A new file can only go inside a shared or followed folder.
  • Each change names the skills it uses. If one is locked, nothing is written and Dum says which skill and why.
  • Dum reads a file before changing it, and writes only if the file is byte-for-byte what it read. If you saved in your editor since, the change is refused and Dum reads it again. It never writes over a save it hasn't seen.
  • A new file is never created over one that appeared in the meantime. Yours is kept.

The diff shows in the conversation or on the handoff card, and More → Changes lists every change in the goal, newest first. Revert puts the file back the way it was, or deletes a file Dum created. It works after the share has ended. If you've edited the file since Dum wrote it, Revert refuses and your edits stay.

Each change is kept in ~/.dum/zones/<zone>/changes/ with the file before, after, and the full patch. open the change record opens the patch.

Dum doesn't run your programs, tests or builds. Run them in your own terminal.

The look

Dum is always on. While you're in a goal it looks every 3 seconds, without a model, and calls the look model whenever the screen changed. The look keeps Dum's picture of what you're doing current; it doesn't interrupt you with tips. It never reads keystrokes, the clipboard or your editor's unsaved text.

What it reads every 3 seconds

  • Apps: which app is in front, its name and window number. Not window titles.
  • Screen: a 320-pixel-wide image of the display your cursor is on, shrunk to a 64 × 40 grid of gray levels and compared with the last one. Cells covered by Dum's own circle, column, panel or bubble are skipped, so the circle moving or animating doesn't count. Only the number of changed cells leaves the main app process. The image isn't kept or saved.
  • Followed folders: whether any file's size or timestamps changed. A file that changed is read in full. New files are picked up about every 15 seconds.

What makes it call the look model

  • Any 3-second look where the screen changed.
  • You saved, added or removed a file in a followed folder, and it stayed quiet for two looks.
  • You switched apps or windows and stayed for two looks.
  • The screen was busy for a bit, then went still.

They all share the same stream of calls and the same caps.

What one call sends

Each call starts fresh. When the screen changed, your look model gets one fresh picture of the display your cursor is on, 1280 pixels wide, with Dum's own windows painted out, and a line about what it saw last, never older pictures. The goal's context goes too. A saved file adds its diff, and an app switch adds the app's name. Nothing you type or copy is sent.

What it keeps

  • The latest observation, in memory only. It isn't saved. Each new note also goes in the Monitor's context log.
  • A short note in the goal's memory, only when what you're doing meaningfully changes: at most one a minute, and never one that nearly repeats the last five.
  • The skills it noticed you working on, as visits on the session's trail. A guess it isn't sure of becomes a gap, never a visit, and none of it counts as evidence.

The look doesn't publish advice.

The caps

  • One call at a time, at least 3 seconds apart.
  • At most 1,200 calls an hour.
  • An identical frame is never sent twice.
  • A call that hasn't answered after 45 seconds is stopped, and it still counts.
  • A minute between file triggers, 90 seconds between typing pauses, two minutes between app triggers, and the same app window doesn't trigger again for 10 minutes.
  • Up to four file diffs and 96 KiB per call. A frame over 3.75 MB isn't sent.

Look calls are billed to your own API key, like every other call. Dum doesn't estimate the cost.

When it doesn't call

No call and no frame while the look is paused, while it's blocked (Dum is answering you or waiting on your answer, you're recording voice, the Mac is asleep or locked, or there's no goal yet), without a backend, while a Dum window is in front, or with the screen look off. With the screen look off, saved files and app switches still call, without a picture.

Screen look and permission

Settings → Look has two switches, Apps and Screen, both on in a new install, with the Screen Recording status and an Open Screen Recording settings button. If you say no to the permission, Dum looks at app switches and saved files only, until you allow it.

Frames also need a look model that is verified for pictures. With haiku or any other unverified Claude model the look runs on text and Settings says No pictures: … isn't verified for pictures yet. Settings → Agent → Verify for pictures (one small call) fixes that; see who powers Dum.

Pausing

Pause and Resume are in the Monitor. It stays paused until you resume or restart Dum. While it's paused the circle shows a dashed ring and the Monitor says Paused.

Followed folders

A followed folder belongs to one goal. Dum may read the saved files in it, notices when you save, and can write changes there. Up to eight folders per goal.

  • Only text files up to 256 KiB are read, up to 2,000 files per folder.
  • Hidden files and folders are skipped, which includes .git and .env, and so are node_modules, dist, build and target.
  • Credential-looking files are skipped: SSH keys, .pem and .key files, token and secret files, and the like. That's by name; a secret pasted into an ordinary source file isn't detected.
  • Your home folder, the whole disk, system folders and ~/Library can't be followed.

Without a backend Dum still watches and sends nothing, and the look's status says no backend.

Noticing app switches, the Screen Recording prompt, the 1280-pixel capture on Retina displays, masking Dum's windows out of real captures, and the real cost per hour haven't been tried on a real Mac yet.

Suggested projects

Dum doesn't run courses or quizzes. When you want practice, or an option says Learn first, it suggests projects that fit a skill's scope and your goal.

  • :projects new suggests projects for this goal; :projects recursion sizes them to one skill. Add in go for a language. The skill tree's Tree view has a suggest projects button on each skill, and More → Suggested projects has Suggest projects for this goal.
  • Each project says what done means, roughly how many active hours it takes, how hard it is, why it fits, and an ordered list of milestones, prerequisites first.
  • Projects are sorted by estimated time, then difficulty. A second model call checks each one covers what it claims; up to four come back.
  • Suggestions use the goal's text and notes, your tree, the goal's memory, and your personal context file if you turned it on in Settings.
  • :projects start p1 marks one you're working on, until you :projects stop or pass every milestone unaided.
  • :submit p1 main.go --unaided hands in your work, as does Hand it in in Suggested projects. Each milestone is reviewed on its own, in prerequisite order. Without --unaided, or the I wrote this myself box, you get the review but no build credit.

Choosing or starting a project unlocks nothing. Time estimates are model guesses.

The Wizard

The Wizard helps you decide. It speaks at goal alignment and when you press Help me decide, as cards in the goal's chat. Dum can also ask it for you: when a message of yours has Dum weighing two or more approaches, Dum hands the choice to the Wizard. The transcript shows Dum asked the Wizard: and the reason, in the Wizard's voice, and a normal decision card lands once Dum's turn ends. That happens at most once per message, and a card you dismiss doesn't come back until you send again. The Wizard doesn't teach, quiz, review your code or post tips you didn't ask for. Dum owns the outcome and the handoff; the Wizard lays out the choices and their tradeoffs, and you choose.

It can only cite context Dum gave it, shown under Based on, and only skills from your tree. When it backs a tradeoff with an outside fact, the claim and the link come from a fixed catalog of primary sources: Git, SQLite, Python, Node and Effective Go docs, the Rust Book, cppreference, MDN and the HTTP RFC. Its own sentences pass a word screen that drops dates, statistics, quotes, unsourced names and claims about what everyone does. The screen is a filter, not a fact-checker. Whether an option can be delegated is decided by Dum's gate, not by the Wizard.

Settings and debug chat

Settings is its own circle in the column and its own panel. It has exactly this, top to bottom:

  • Agent: the backend, the three models and your API key. A Claude model that isn't verified for pictures has a Verify for pictures (one small call) button next to its role; see who powers Dum.
  • Look: the Apps and Screen switches, Screen Recording status and Open Screen Recording settings.
  • Mode: Understand or Anti-vibe; see talking to Dum.
  • Shortcuts: Open Dum, Hold to talk and Send the draft. Click a field and press the new shortcut.
  • Move circle: pick a display, or nudge it with the arrow keys.
  • Open at login: starts the circle and Dum's host, not a panel.
  • Use personal context: background about you that shapes decisions and suggested projects, with the files it read. It never adds skills.
  • Set up voice, with its status.
  • Debug chat.
  • The version, and Quit Dum.

The sections start closed, except one that needs fixing: no backend chosen, a shortcut that couldn't be set, or Screen Recording denied. Pause is in the Monitor, and the web tree is in the skill tree's Tree view.

Debug chat

Debug chat answers questions about Dum itself, like which look model is running? or why didn't the look call just now?. It reads Dum's status, its recent events and a short reference, and nothing else. It can't change settings, goals, files or skills, never sees your goal's conversation, and works with no goal at all. It has Send, Stop, New debug session and Clear.

  • Each Send is one call to your chosen backend. Without one, it says so and opens Agent.
  • The events it reads are kept in memory only: the last 500, 512 KiB, or 30 minutes, whichever is smallest. Keys, tokens and provider error bodies are never recorded, and anything that looks like a key is redacted from your question and the reply.
  • Nothing is saved to disk. A debug session expires after 30 idle minutes.

Who powers Dum

Who powers Dum? picks the backend and three models: Dum's model for the conversation and handoffs, the Helper model for the Wizard, screenshots and project suggestions, and the Look model for the look. All three come from the same backend. The look model has to take pictures. Change them later under Settings → Agent.

Backends

  • Claude. Your own Anthropic API key, in every build. That's the only way Dum connects to Claude. Defaults are opus at high effort for Dum, fable at high effort for the helper and haiku at low effort for the look. Dum ships with its own Claude Code, so you don't install anything.
  • ChatGPT. Built, but switched off in current builds.

Model lists come live from the backend. For Claude that means the ids Claude's catalog lists, including aliases like opus, fable and haiku, each of which runs some specific model today. Dum verifies models by that resolved model, not the alias. Dum's model has to support function calling. Pictures, for the look and shared screenshots, go only to a model whose resolved id is verified for pictures. Models Dum hasn't verified are marked untested, and the look runs on text with them; its status says why.

opus and fable are verified out of the box. For any other Claude model, haiku included, Settings → Agent shows Verify for pictures (one small call) next to that role. It sends one 2×2 PNG and the line Reply with the single word OK. to that model on your key. Success records the resolved id in ~/.dum/verified-models.json on this Mac, and the look starts sending frames. Failure records nothing and the reason stays next to the button. Dum won't verify while it's mid-request. If an alias later moves to a model you haven't verified, pictures stop until you verify that one.

An Anthropic key has to be scoped to a workspace. A key created outside one fails with must include the anthropic-workspace-id header; Dum doesn't add that header. Create the key inside a workspace at console.anthropic.com.

There's no fallback. If the backend you picked fails, Dum tells you; it never quietly switches to another one.

Your API key is encrypted with Electron's safe storage, which uses the macOS Keychain, and kept in Dum's app data, never in settings.

Key encryption hasn't been tried on a real Mac yet.

Privacy

What stays on your Mac

  • Your skill tree, goals, conversations, memory, evidence, directions, handoffs, sessions, story, suggested projects and change records, all as plain files in ~/.dum/.
  • Settings, where the circle sits, your pinned goals, and the encrypted API key, in Dum's app data folder.
  • Voice: audio and transcription never leave the Mac.
  • The look's routine screen samples. Only frames sent with a model call leave, and none are saved.
  • Debug events, in memory only.

What goes to the backend

Your messages, the goal's text and notes, your skills and boundary, recent conversation, and the text of files Dum reads from shares and followed folders. For goal alignment and decision cards: the goal, the agreed direction, the context they cite (notes, memory, the latest observation, recent conversation) and the skills that could apply. For the look: the goal's context, the previous observation in words, the front app's name, diffs of saved files, and one fresh screen frame per call while the screen look is on. For decisions and suggestions: your personal context file, if you turned it on. For debug chat: your question and Dum's redacted diagnostics, never goal data.

  • Claude: sent to Anthropic, through Claude Code, under your own API key.

The web tree is off unless you link it in the skill tree's Tree view → Web tree. Then only your skills and removals go to that server: no goals, sessions, story, conversations, holds or files. Anyone with the link can see and edit the tree there.
