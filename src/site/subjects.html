Other subjects

A zone is whatever you're learning, so it doesn't have to be code. Make one called Math › Real analysis or Music › Arranging, write its goal in your own words, and Dum keeps that zone's conversation, memory and notes apart from the rest. The skill tree and evidence are still built around programming.

We're exploring subject-specific skill trees and evidence. The examples below describe what works today and where that could go, not a finished assessment system for every subject.

What carries over

Zones. Nest them the way the subject splits, like History › Reconstruction › Primary sources. A zone's goal and Notes for this zone reach the zones inside it, so background you write once applies to the whole branch.

Conversation. Dum can discuss a proof, a score or a historical source. The prompts and the Wizard's source catalog are programming-oriented, and a plausible answer isn't a subject-specific assessment.

Memory. :remember keeps a note in the zone's memory, and it comes back next time you're in that zone. Use it for decisions, open questions and what you've already tried.

Your files. Share a LaTeX file, a LilyPond score or an essay draft with your message, or follow its folder in the zone, and Dum can read it. :inspect shows Dum part of a file you shared or follow. When you save in a followed folder, the look notices and keeps Dum's picture of what you're working on current. It doesn't comment.

The screen. With the screen look on, the look model gets a fresh frame of your screen when it changes, so Dum's context can include a diagram, a score or a scanned page you have open. You can also send a screenshot of a window on purpose.

Deciding what to do next. Goal alignment and Help me decide work for any goal: Dum reflects what you're after, and the Wizard compares a few ways to get there, including learning projects. A handoff still has to pass the skill gate and ends in file changes, and both of those were built for code.

What's still built for code

The curated tracks are programming: language basics, plus graphics, interview, systems and a builder track. The three evidence levels (recognize, build, apply) and project hand-ins were designed for code.

You can add custom skills, but there are no curated math, music or history tracks and no validated standards for them. A skill note doesn't establish that a proof, a performance or a historical claim is sound.

For each subject the open questions are what independent work would demonstrate a skill, how prerequisites fit together, and which sources make a review checkable. Suggested projects should stay substantial rather than turning every topic into a short quiz.

Mathematics

Working through a proof, a problem set or a paper? Dum can discuss approaches, follow the steps of an argument, and remember context across sessions in the zone. Ask it whether an argument has a gap. Its judgment can be wrong, so check its reasoning.

What it looks like

  • Follow the folder with your LaTeX source; ask about a specific step, or ask Dum to follow the argument.
  • Describe the structure of a proof and get feedback on whether the cases look complete.
  • Keep the approaches you've ruled out in the zone's memory so Dum doesn't suggest them again.
  • Send a screenshot of a plot or diagram and ask what Dum sees.

Current limits

There's no curated math track and no proof checker. A useful direction is a proof notebook that spans several techniques, where completed proofs and counterexamples would be the evidence, not a confident explanation alone.

Music

Composing, arranging, transcribing or analyzing? Dum can discuss theory questions, arrangement choices and structure. Share notation in a text format and ask about it.

What it looks like

  • Share LilyPond, ABC or a plain-text lead sheet; discuss a specific bar or harmonic move.
  • Describe what you're going for and ask about common approaches in that style or period.
  • Keep compositional decisions in the zone's memory: why you chose a form, what you ruled out and why.
  • Send a screenshot of a score or DAW view and ask for observations.

Current limits

There's no score or performance assessment. A candidate project is an arrangement with an annotated score and a recorded performance, with separate evidence for harmony, voice leading and interpretation. Text and screen advice can't verify a performance or replace a musician's ear.

History and research

Writing a research paper, reading primary sources, or building a historical argument? Dum can discuss interpretations, source context and essay structure, and the zone's memory keeps it useful across many sessions on one topic.

What it looks like

  • Share a draft paragraph; ask about clarity or the structure of the argument.
  • Describe a source; discuss its likely provenance, its limits and how to read it critically.
  • Keep research threads, working hypotheses and sources you've already checked in the zone's memory.
  • Send a screenshot of an archival image or a dense passage and ask Dum to describe what it sees.

Current limits

There's no scholarly-source verification and no curated history track. A substantial direction is a sourced essay or a small critical edition, where primary-source comparisons, traceable citations and argued interpretations would each be separate evidence. Check dates and provenance yourself; a model agreeing with you isn't historical proof.

The look and voice

The look is on by default. Every 3 seconds Dum checks which app is in front, how much the screen changed and whether files in followed folders were saved, and it calls a model only when something changed. You can pause it in Current context or turn the screen part off in Settings. See the look.

Voice is deliberate. You hold the talk shortcut, and the words go into your draft on your Mac. Nothing is sent until you send it. The look pauses while you're recording.
